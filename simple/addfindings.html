Addition-with-flags on the Goldmont microcode engine

Celeron N3350, red-unlocked · vmwrite (0x0cd8) hook → patch RAM U7c00 · results from additionwithflags.c · 45 / 45 certifying checks pass, 27 / 28 exploratory probes match.

The two-domain flag model (confirmed end-to-end). The microcode engine exposes two independent flag stores. Domain #1 is a per-register latch of the arithmetic flags written by every ALU op into its destination register and read back by SETCC_COND*. Domain #2 is the architectural RFLAGS.CF that the caller sets with popfq before the trigger; it is the only carry-in ADC ever reads, and it is frozen at patch entry. The two are isolated: a carry produced in Domain #1 is invisible to ADC unless GENARITHFLAGS_RR(TMP,TMP) explicitly copies it across.

Section 1 — ADD + SETCC carry chain (Domain #1) 6/6 PASS

This is the carry primitive every production curve uses, in isolation: ADD → TMP deposits a carry in that register's Domain #1 latch, SETCC_CONDB materialises it as a 0/1 value, and a folding ADD adds it into the next limb — no ADC, no architectural flag touched. The 128-bit adder built this way reproduced __uint128_t exactly on every edge case (zero, no-carry, low-limb overflow propagating into the high limb, and a full wrap where the 129th-bit carry is correctly dropped) and on 5 000 random inputs. This certifies the production mechanism: carries can be generated, captured, and propagated entirely inside the microcode domain with no dependence on architectural RFLAGS.

Section 2 — ADC reads the frozen architectural CF (Domain #2) 10/10 PASS

2a (single ADC). With both source operands zero, ADC returned exactly the entry CF (0→0, 1→1), and 5+3 returned 8 or 9 depending on entry CF. So ADC's carry-in is unambiguously the architectural carry flag.

2b (intra-triad) and 2c (cross-triad). An overflowing ADD placed in slot 0 of the same triad as the ADC (2b), or in the preceding triad (2c), did not change what ADC saw: with entry CF=0 the ADC still read 0, and with a non-overflowing ADD but entry CF=1 it still read 1. Neither slot adjacency nor a triad boundary lets an ADD's carry reach ADC.

2d (two ADCs). The decisive isolation test. Under entry CF=1 the first ADC returned 1 (its own carry-out being 0), and the second ADC also returned 1 — it read the same frozen architectural carry, not the first ADC's output. This proves ADC never writes Domain #2: the architectural CF is constant for the lifetime of the patch unless something deliberately rewrites it.

Section 3 — Negative controls: can ADC reach a Domain-#1 carry? 7/7 PASS

This section answers the central question by isolating cause from effect, with the entry architectural CF forced so that any 1 from ADC could only have come from the microcode-domain carry.

  • (A) the carry genuinely exists. After an overflowing ADD, SETCC read the carry as 1 (and 0 for a non-overflowing add). So the Domain #1 carry is real and present.
  • (B) ADC cannot see it. With that same Domain #1 carry set and entry CF=0, ADC returned 0 — not because the carry is absent (A proves it is present) but because ADC simply cannot reach it.
  • (C) SETCC is not a bridge. Inserting a SETCC that reads the carry before the ADC changed nothing: ADC still returned 0. Reading the carry does not promote it to the architectural flag.
  • (D) GENARITHFLAGS_RR is the bridge. The only change that made ADC return 1 was inserting GENARITHFLAGS_RR(TMP0,TMP0) between the ADD and the ADC.
  • (E) the carry-in is immovable. Setting and then overwriting the Domain #1 carry (1→0 and 0→1) never altered what ADC read — it always returned the frozen entry value. The two domains do not leak into each other in either direction.

Answer to "without GENARITHFLAGS, can ADC find a microcode-domain carry?" — No. And SETCC does not substitute for the bridge; GENARITHFLAGS_RR(TMP,TMP) is required.

Section 4 — GENARITHFLAGS_RR bridges Domain #1 → #2 10/10 PASS

The bridge was exercised over eight inputs spanning no-overflow and overflow. The crucial cases are the discriminators — overflows whose low word is non-zero (FF..FE+3, FF..FE+5, and a random pair). For each, ADC read CF=1, proving the bridge copies the actual carry and not a coincidental "result == 0" condition (a confound in the earliest 2026-04 measurements, now ruled out). The two appended checks confirm the bridge writes the architectural flag rather than OR-ing into it: starting from entry CF=1, a non-overflowing add drove CF back to 0. That write-not-OR behaviour is what makes a multi-limb chain able to propagate a "no-carry" limb correctly.

Section 5 — ADC's carry-out is itself a Domain-#1 carry (chainable) 5/5 PASS

ADC's destination register receives a Domain #1 carry exactly like an ADD's does, so a second GENARITHFLAGS_RR on that register re-publishes it to the architectural flag for the next ADC. Across the five cases (first add carrying or not, second add overflowing or not) the re-bridged carry was always correct. This is what makes the GENARITHFLAGS_RR + ADC chain compose to arbitrary width at two micro-ops per limb.

Section 6 — end-to-end 256-bit add (chained ADC + bridge) 5/5 PASS

The full {ADC, GENARITHFLAGS_RR(dst,dst)} packed layout — the same one used in production — computed a 4×64 addition (result and final carry) matching __uint128_t on the edge vectors (zero, single-limb carry, carry rippling through all four limbs, max + max) and on 10 000 random inputs. This is the working primitive, not just the building blocks: the bridge is reliable inside a realistic multi-triad patch.

Section 7 — Domain #1 latches the FULL flag set NEW · 24/24 MATCH

Headline finding. The per-register flag latch is not CF-only. Reading TMP0 after an ADD through SETCC_CONDB, _CONDZ, _CONDS and _CONDO reproduced the x86 CF, ZF, SF and OF on all six vectors — CF 6/6, ZF 6/6, SF 6/6, OF 6/6. The microcode domain carries the complete arithmetic flag set per register, even though production code has only ever used CF.

The INT64_MAX+1 and INT64_MIN+INT64_MIN rows are the discriminators: they separate signed overflow (OF) from unsigned carry (CF), and the engine got both independently right. The practical consequence is that zero-detection, sign tests, and signed-overflow detection — hence conditional reduction and constant-time comparison — are all expressible directly in the microcode domain via SETCC_CONDZ/CONDS/CONDO, with no architectural-flag round trip.

Section 8 — per-register carry lifetime #6 PASS #5 model refined

#6 — SETCC reads are non-destructive. Two consecutive SETCC_CONDB reads of the same register's carry both returned 1; reading the latch does not consume it.

#5 — what (re)writes a register's latched carry. Starting from TMP0 holding an ADD carry of 1, we overwrote TMP0 with a different op and re-read its carry:

The one probe that "differed" from the stated hypothesis (ZEROEXT) is not an error — it refines the model. Unlike an x86 MOV, which is flag-transparent, a microcode ZEROEXT rewrites its destination's Domain #1 flags to "no carry." Combined with the other three rows, the rule is clean: a register's latched flags are redefined by whatever op most recently wrote that register — arithmetic ops by their result, SHL/SHR by the shifted-out bit, and ZEROEXT/OR to zero. Two operational corollaries for code generation: (1) a carry must be captured with SETCC before any other op overwrites that register, which is exactly what the production curve25519 schedule does; and (2) operations on other registers never disturb a carry you are holding, which is what lets the squaring routine compute shifts of the accumulator into scratch registers without losing the limb carry.

Summary for the paper

The microcode ALU maintains a per-register arithmetic-flag latch (CF, ZF, SF, OF) that is the substrate of all our carry handling, and a separate, entry-frozen architectural carry that ADC/SBB consume. The two are strictly isolated, and the only documented bridge from the register domain to the architectural domain is GENARITHFLAGS_RR(TMP,TMP), which performs a genuine write of the real carry (not an OR, not a result-is-zero proxy). SETCC reads the register domain but does not bridge to ADC. Two carry-chain styles follow, both verified correct: ADD + SETCC + fold at three ops per limb (never touching architectural flags, used in production), and GENARITHFLAGS_RR + ADC at two ops per limb (routing through the architectural carry). The newly confirmed full-flag latch additionally opens signed-comparison and zero-detection primitives in the microcode domain.
