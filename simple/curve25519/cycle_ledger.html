1 — What a kernel number contains

The published kernel cost includes the cost of getting into the patch

A microcode field operation is not just the patch body. The caller marshals ten limbs into fixed architectural registers, executes the hooked instruction, waits for the sequencer to redirect into patch RAM, and stores five result limbs back. All of that is inside the 102.8 and 81.8 figures.

The harness measures the overhead separately by firing the same wrapper at a one-triad no-op patch. That is the floor — everything except the arithmetic:

Subtracting that floor from your own number and comparing the remainder against OpenSSL's full operation would be cheating — OpenSSL also pays a call, and also loads ten limbs and stores five. So the harness now measures a native floor too: the same call shape, the same memory traffic, no arithmetic.

The two floors are not the same shape. The microcode floor is flat at 16.2 for both operations, because redirection costs the same no matter how much data you hand it. The native floor scales with operand traffic — 10.1 for a multiply's ten loads, 6.4 for a square's five. The microcode-specific penalty is therefore 6.1 cycles on a multiply and 9.8 on a square.

Quote the multiply, not the square

The fe_sq row flatters you and must not be quoted. FE_SQ's lea/imul precompute (2·ai and 19·ai) sits inside the microcode floor, so subtracting the floor removes that arithmetic from your side — while OpenSSL's fe51_sqr does its doubling inside the routine and keeps paying for it. fe_mul has no such asymmetry: your 19·bj precompute is inside the patch (IMUL64L in PREP), so both sides carry their own. 0.996× is the defensible number; 0.982× is not.

3 — Scaling one operation to one X25519

The kernel-level deficit is entirely invocation cost

One X25519 issues 1,287 multiplications and 1,274 squarings — 255 ladder steps at five and four, plus 254 squarings and 12 multiplications in the inversion and final conversion. That is 2,561 firings, each paying the entry cost once.

The decomposition closes to 0.0%. At the level of field operations you can say exactly where every cycle of the OpenSSL gap comes from, and none of it is the arithmetic. This is a stronger result than a narrow win would have been: a win invites the reply “your baseline was weak”; an accounting identity invites nothing.

It is also the mechanism that ties the two case studies together. Entry cost is fixed per firing, so the benefit of microcode scales with work per firing. Keccak amortises one 16-cycle entry over 1,396 triads and wins by 1.084×. X25519 pays that entry 2,561 times and ties. Same curve, two ends.

The non-field residue — everything that is not a field operation — is 47,174 cycles for microcode against 44,340 for OpenSSL. On an identical ladder those should match, and 2,833 apart is what matching looks like. The entry-cost account therefore survives the step from one operation to a whole scalar multiplication, which is the claim that matters.

Retracted — a measurement error, not an effect

An earlier version of this page reported ≈27,000 cycles of this gap as unexplained, and offered hook-switching and working-set hypotheses for it. Both were tested and refuted: alternating mul/sq costs +0.2 cycles against a homogeneous chain, and rotating through five field slots instead of two costs +0.0. The residue was an artefact of comparing current kernel measurements against a sweep from 2026-09-09 that predated the five-accumulator fe_mul. With both measured on the same source it is 802 cycles, not 27,000.

5 — Standing

End-to-end, the implementation places fifth

Complete implementations, each at its own best compiler configuration. These differ in representation, ladder structure, inversion and integration all at once, so the table establishes position and attributes nothing.

The 2026-09-09 sweep put this work at 302 kcycles and 10.5% behind amd64-64. It had measured the previous 66-triad serial fe_mul; the 58-triad five-accumulator rewrite was sitting uncommitted in the working tree and had never been swept. Re-running it moved every 5×51 microcode contender by 22–26k cycles and nothing else by more than 0.3%.

Against OpenSSL on the identical ladder, microcode is ahead in 0 of 24 compiler configurations — paired geometric mean 0.843×. This is a clean sweep, not a close call, and any framing that implies a configuration exists where you win will be checked and will fail.

These three contenders were measured on every sweep all along. They were dropped by a stale column list in the table generator — zip() truncating a 15-column table against a 12-name header — and never reached the tables. That is fixed, with an assertion so it cannot recur silently.

6 — Presentation

How to say this

Say

Arithmetic parity with the fastest hand-tuned assembly

“With each side's invocation cost removed, the microcode field multiplication is within 0.4% of OpenSSL's hand-written fe51 assembly.” Parity, never a win — 0.4% on a derived quantity evaporates if a reviewer disputes the floor by two cycles, and it buys you nothing.

Say

1.4–1.75× over everything else, on all 24 configurations

fiat-crypto, CryptOpt, amd64-51 assembly and hand-written C, beaten on both operations at kernel level and on every matched compiler configuration end-to-end. This is your strongest unqualified result and it does not depend on the floor argument at all.

Say

Entry cost is the finding, not the excuse

A flat 16.2 cycles to enter patch RAM against 10.1 for a native call, paid 2,561 times, decomposing the deficit to 0.0%. It explains Keccak's win and X25519's tie with one mechanism — benefit scales with work per firing. Two case studies, one curve, measured at both ends.

Avoid

“Fastest 5×51” or “lowest kernel cost”

Both are false with OpenSSL in the table, and OpenSSL is the first baseline any reviewer will ask for. It has been in your benchmark binary the whole time, which makes the omission harder to explain than the loss.

Avoid

Quoting the fe_sq parity figure

0.982× is an artefact of where the squaring precompute is charged. Use fe_mul, or state the asymmetry in the same breath.

Say

The account survives the step to a full scalar multiplication

The invocation differential predicts 20,285 cycles; the measured same-ladder gap is 21,087. Level 1 and Level 2 agree to 96%, and the non-field residue matches to 2,833 cycles on an identical ladder. This is the sentence that makes the entry-cost model a result rather than a rationalisation.

Measurement. Intel Celeron N3350, Goldmont, core 0, taskset -c 0. Turbo disabled (no_turbo=1), userspace governor at 1094400 kHz; delivered core 1100 MHz against a 1094 MHz TSC, so RDTSC ticks × 1.00548 = core cycles. That correction is common-mode and every ratio on this page is invariant to it.
