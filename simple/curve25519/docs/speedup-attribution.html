Where the Speed-up Comes From

Attributing the microcode field-arithmetic advantage on Intel Goldmont — a dispatch-floor effect, not an arithmetic-structure effect.

Thesis. A microcode field operation on this microarchitecture behaves, to first order, as a fixed-latency primitive. Its invocation cost is governed by microcode-invocation overhead together with a critical path fixed by the field representation, and is essentially independent of the micro-op schedule. The same-representation advantage over a native software baseline is therefore the position of this invocation floor relative to the native routine's latency — not the cleverness of the field-arithmetic encoding.

This note documents the experiments that localize the performance difference between our microcode field arithmetic for X25519 and the fastest native scalar baselines on the Intel Goldmont N3350 (Celeron). It is written to be lifted, with minor notational alignment, into a paper's analysis section. Absolute cycle figures below should be re-collected with the core frequency pinned for a camera-ready; the ratios and conclusions are stable across frequency.

1. A performance model for microcode field operations

We model the latency of a single field-operation invocation, measured in a dependency chain (each invocation consuming its predecessor's output, as in the Montgomery ladder), as

Tfire  =  Oinv  +  Lcrit

where Oinv is the fixed overhead of entering and leaving the patched microcode sequencer — decoding the hooking x86 instruction, sequencer entry and exit, micro-op issue, architectural write-back, and the stall incurred by the dependent consumer — and Lcrit is the critical-path latency of the field operation's micro-op dataflow graph. A triad denotes a microcode word of three micro-operations issued as a unit.

On the Goldmont N3350 we measure Tfire ≈ 123–127 cycles for both fe_mul and fe_sq, despite their differing sizes (66 vs. 42 triads). The near-equality of two operations of substantially different micro-op count is the first indication that Tfire is governed by Oinv together with a critical path Lcrit that is fixed by the field representation rather than by the instruction schedule.

Measurement regime Independent-invocation microbenchmarks report lower per-operation figures (e.g. ≈ 58 cyc for fe_mul) because successive firings pipeline. The Montgomery ladder is dependency-bound and exposes the full Tfire; we report the dependency-bound figure as the operationally relevant cost and state the regime for every number.

2. Isolating the source of the advantage

The same-representation speed-up of the microcode field arithmetic over the native baseline is a geometric-mean factor of 1.069 over the unsaturated 5×51 amd64-51 routines, measured by substituting only the field-operation backend into a fixed ladder, inversion, and conditional-swap framework (a same-ladder design that isolates the field-op contribution). To attribute this factor, we performed three controlled ablations, each varying a single structural property of the patch while holding the field mathematics, the calling wrapper, and the dependency-bound harness constant.

2.1 The multiplier

A microcode patch issuing an isolated dependent chain of multiplies reproduces the latency of the native mul instruction to within measurement error (5.463 vs. 5.464 cycles per multiply, identical results). The microcode and native paths invoke the same hardware multiplier at the same latency; the multiply primitive contributes no advantage.

2.2 Carry-propagation topology

A pair of patches equal in micro-op and triad count, differing only in carry-dependency depth — a single serial accumulator versus four independent accumulator lanes carried in the per-register flag domain — exhibit no measurable latency difference. The flag-domain carry handling, although it removes the architectural-flag (adc) serialization inherent to a native carry chain, does not lower the invocation latency.

2.3 Schedule density

Exploiting the fully sequential intra-triad execution semantics of the target, we re-emit the production fe_mul micro-op stream at reduced packing, increasing the issue-slot count by 50% while leaving the computation provably unchanged (verified differentially against an independent reference over 2000 inputs). Latency rises by only ≈ 4%.

Common result Across all three ablations, ∂Tfire / ∂(schedule) ≈ 0. The microcode field operation is, to first order, a fixed-latency primitive: its cost is set by the invocation overhead Oinv and by a critical path Lcrit determined by the limb-carry recurrence of the chosen radix, and is essentially independent of the micro-op schedule that realizes it.

3. Interpretation: a single mechanism for both directions

The schedule-level techniques that distinguish our patches from a naïve encoding — progressive accumulation, parallel carry lanes, dense triad packing — operate exclusively on micro-operations that lie off the latency-critical path (the partial-product multiply–accumulates), which the wide microcode register file already overlaps beneath the carry recurrence and the invocation overhead. They reduce the number of issue slots, and hence the patch size, but not Tfire.

Consequently, the performance difference between the microcode implementation and a native software field operation reduces to the position of the invocation floor relative to the native routine's latency:

  • Versus amd64-51, which uses the same unsaturated 5×51 representation and therefore the same arithmetic critical path Lcrit, the advantage is precisely that the microcode invocation floor Oinv + Lcrit undercuts the cost of the native routine — function-call overhead plus an architectural-flag-serialized carry chain on a multiplier- and ADX-constrained Atom core. The 6.9% same-ladder speed-up is a dispatch-floor effect, not an arithmetic-structure effect.
  • Versus amd64-64, which uses a saturated 4×64 representation, the native field operation has a shorter carry recurrence (fewer, wider limbs) and pays no invocation overhead; its latency lies below our floor, and the microcode implementation does not win. The only levers that could close this gap are reducing Oinv — not exposed to the patch author — or shortening Lcrit by changing the representation, i.e. the same lever that separates amd64-64 from amd64-51.
Packing is a capacity constraint, not an optimization Triad packing, although immaterial to latency, remains necessary: the fully unpacked fe_mul requires 198 triads and does not fit the 128-triad patch-RAM bound. Packing buys the ability to fit the operation (and to co-resident fe_mul with fe_sq under the cap), not speed — an observation we believe is characteristic of programming in patch RAM more generally.

4. Measurement methodology

  • Same-ladder isolation. Field-op backends are substituted into one fixed framework (ladder, inversion, conditional swap), so a measured difference reflects the field operation alone.
  • Differential verification. Every patch is checked bit-for-bit against an independent schoolbook reference and an optimized __uint128_t implementation over hand-chosen edge vectors, thousands of random inputs, and an iterated chain, before any timing.
  • Timing. Minimum of many same-process, back-to-back repetitions; contenders are compared within one process so they share a frequency. On Goldmont rdtsc advances at a fixed reference rate distinct from the (turbo-variable) core clock, so absolute tick counts are interpreted only relative to a pinned baseline, and cross-implementation ratios are treated as the primary result.
  • Regime. Dependency-bound (ladder-representative) figures are reported for end-to-end relevance; throughput figures, where given, are labelled as such.
