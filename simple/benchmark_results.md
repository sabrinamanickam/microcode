# X25519 Benchmark Results

CPU: Intel Celeron N3350 (Goldmont).
Methodology: 8-config SUPERCOP flag matrix (`gcc`/`clang` × `-O3`/`-O2`/`-Os`/`-O`),
100 repetitions per config. Driver: `bench_supercop_matrix.sh`.
All six implementations pass the RFC 7748 1000-iteration chain test.

Both `min` and `median` are reported. `median ≈ min` everywhere (within
~0.1%), so interrupt jitter is not contaminating these numbers.

## Naming legend

Every contender label has the form **`ladder / field-op-backend`**:

| label              | ladder source         | field-op kernel                                       |
|--------------------|-----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| `ours/hand-C`      | our ladder (this repo)| our hand-written portable C with `__uint128_t`        |
| `ours/fiat`        | our ladder (this repo)| fiat-crypto autogenerated C (Coq-verified)            |
| `ours/ucode`       | our ladder (this repo)| our Goldmont microcode patches                        |
| `donna_c64`        | donna's               | donna's whole-stack portable C                        |
| `amd64-51/asm`     | SUPERCOP amd64-51     | Bernstein/Schwabe whole-stack hand-tuned x86-64 asm   |
| `amd64-51/ucode`   | SUPERCOP amd64-51     | our microcode field ops in amd64-51's framework       |

"Same-ladder" tables hold the ladder constant and vary only the field-op
backend — those are the comparisons where the field-op kernel is what's
actually being measured.

## Interpretation

1. **Cleanest claim (table b)**: Microcode field arithmetic is **~5.25%
   faster** than amd64-51's hand-tuned x86-64 assembly field arithmetic
   on Goldmont N3350, when both are run inside amd64-51's own driver.
   Same flags, same CPU, same measurement methodology — every variable
   except `fe25519_mul`/`fe25519_square` is held constant.

2. **End-to-end (table c)**: `ours/ucode` (150 292 cyc, clang -O2) is
   **~6.4% faster than `donna_c64`** (160 553, the next-fastest
   contender on this CPU) and **~11.7% faster than `amd64-51/asm`**
   (170 253).

3. **Our ladder vs amd64-51's framework**: Compare `ours/ucode`
   (150 292) to `amd64-51/ucode` (161 306). Same field ops; only the
   surrounding framework changes. **~11k cycles delta in favour of our
   framework.** Most plausible cause: the amd64-51 hybrid pays a
   `ladderstep()` function-call overhead (255 calls × ~40 cyc ≈ 10k)
   that we avoid by inlining the ladderstep body directly in the bit
   loop. The amd64-51 design was tuned around a 7000-line inlined-asm
   ladderstep — once you replace it with a separate C function, the
   framework loses its primary advantage.

4. **Compiler picks matter for pure C, not for asm/microcode**:
   - **Pure C** (`hand-C`, `fiat`, `donna_c64`): result varies by 5–35%
     across compiler/flag configs. Best is sometimes gcc -O3, sometimes
     clang. `donna_c64` specifically does badly under clang on Goldmont
     (~210k vs 160k under gcc -O3) — a real outlier.
   - **`amd64-51/asm`, `amd64-51/ucode`, `ours/ucode`**: nearly flat
     across configs (variance ~1–2%), because the hot path is asm or
     microcode that the C compiler doesn't see. The variance that does
     remain is C glue (mont25519, ladderstep wrapper, etc.).

## Caveats (from `benchmark_review.md`)

- `amd64-51/asm`'s 170k figure is high — published Goldmont/Apollo Lake
  numbers are typically 130–150k. Likely cause: CPU frequency scaling
  not pinned. Pin before final numbers:
  ```
  sudo cpupower frequency-set -g performance
  ```
  Relative ordering should be stable; absolute numbers may shift down.
- All `min`/`median` pairs agree to within 30 cycles, confirming that
  interrupt jitter and other noise are not contaminating the results.
